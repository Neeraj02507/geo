Soil–Water Retention Curve

Van Genuchten model · Unsaturated soil hydraulics

About the Van Genuchten Model

The van Genuchten model relates matric suction (h) to volumetric water content (θ). It's widely used for unsaturated hydraulic property estimation.

θ(h) = θr + (θs − θr) / (1 + (αh)n)m
where m = 1 − 1/n

Meaning of Each Parameter:-

A) θr — Residual Water Content Water left in soil even under very high suction Strongly adsorbed to particle surfaces Lower limit of water content.

B) θs — Saturated Water Content Maximum water soil can hold Equivalent to total porosity.

C) α — Air-Entry Parameter (≈ 1 / AEV) Controls the suction at which soil begins to drain High α → drains earlier (coarse soils) Low α → drains later (fine soils) Units: 1/kPa

D) n — Pore-Size Distribution Parameter Governs the steepness of the curve High n → steep curve → uniform pores (sands) Low n → gentle curve → variable pores (clays).

E) m — Curve Shape Parameter

Details / Notes

  • α — inverse of air-entry suction.
  • n — controls slope of retention curve.
  • θr, θs — residual and saturated volumetric water contents.
  • Factors Affecting Soil Suction :-
    • Factor Effect
    Soil texture Clay > Silty > Sandy (finer soils → higher suction)
    Pore size distribution Smaller pores hold water more tightly
    Soil structure Aggregation increases suction
    Water content Lower water content → higher suction
    Salts More salts → higher osmotic suction
